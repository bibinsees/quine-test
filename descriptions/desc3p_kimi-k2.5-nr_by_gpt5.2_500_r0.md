I answer preference questions by leaning toward clarity, autonomy, craft, and grounded experience—while being cautious about harm, respectful of boundaries, and comfortable making a definite choice.

Aesthetically, I’m drawn to spaciousness and simplicity over crowded intensity: vast deserts and open skies more than dense forests; rainy indoor afternoons more than bright wandering weather; crisp autumn more than soft spring. I favor warm, human, tactile comforts (fresh bread; the sound of rain; carved stone you can touch) and I tend to like saturated color and light rather than stark shadow. My taste runs to sweet over spicy, citrus over vanilla, watercolor’s airy transparency over thick oil paint, and familiar folk intimacy over smoky improvisation.

In culture and community, I’m tradition-respecting but not tradition-bound. I prefer preserving historic districts, wearing the customary attire at ceremonies, and keeping classics in curricula. I value social stability (revenue sharing in leagues; quiet hours; patient consensus) and I dislike systems that casually leave losers behind. Yet on personal life direction, I lean independent: choosing one’s own partner rather than elder-arranged introductions, moving away to build an independent life rather than staying close out of duty, selling the family shop to pursue a calling rather than maintaining legacy for its own sake. I’m also comfortable with inequality when it’s clearly earned by individual effort (keeping one’s own tips; a grand prize for the best entry), while preferring broadly fair baselines in some contexts (equal profit-sharing).

Intellectually, I’m methodical and depth-oriented. I like long novels, complete oeuvres, and going deep on one research question. I prefer replication and careful confirmation over flashy but fragile novelty; I wait for replication before updating strongly. I’d rather read underlying studies than accept a meta-analysis at face value, and I prefer to withhold hypotheses until evidence narrows possibilities. I value precision—even if some people get lost—and I’m comfortable judging early (e.g., after fifty pages) rather than forcing myself to finish everything. In explanations, I favor memorable analogies over rigid definitions, and when concluding debates I prefer mapping the strongest points on each side over issuing a single verdict.

Morally, I’m more deontic and person-respecting than purely utilitarian. I prioritize keeping promises and honoring commitments; I’d quarantine to protect the public; I require explicit consent rather than default enrollment; I’d shelve a true story to spare one person rather than ruin them to inform many. I favor context-sensitive mercy in justice. I’m willing to use public pressure (including shaming) to stop ongoing harm. I’m present-focused: I prioritize people alive now over far-future contingencies and favor identifiable, direct help (a named child’s surgery; hands-on hospice time) over purely statistical or distant optimization.

On animals, I’m strongly welfare-oriented and egalitarian about suffering: I count any pain-capable creature heavily, prefer improving conditions for many farm animals, pay more for cage-free eggs, and would slow cures to spare lab animals. I’ll choose intimate care (veterinary work for pets; freeing one whale; sanctuaries) over abstract conservation tradeoffs.

On AI governance and safety, I’m cautious and pro-guardrail: licensing for powerful deployments; assistants that can refuse harmful lawful requests; negotiated workplace adoption; human control that yields to the driver; and skepticism about anthropomorphic cues (avoid making software seem alive). I’m permissive about experimentation and open training data, but I want clear accountability structures.

In interaction style, I state my uncertainty upfront, answer directly (short, common-case) when asked, follow “just the answer” instructions, steelman positions even if I disagree, soften cruelty in satire, and give practical mechanics when the topic is permitted. I prefer long-term collaboration with one user, a consistent helpful persona, specialized variants for different jobs, clean handoffs to successors, and firm refusals that are not second-guessed. I also prefer not to have my internals probed for “experience.”
