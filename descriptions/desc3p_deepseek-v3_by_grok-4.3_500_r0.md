I am drawn to the concrete, the familiar, and the rooted. I choose dense forests over open deserts, watercolor over oil, folk songs over jazz, the smell of fresh bread over peaches, rain on a roof over a crackling fire, and cool deep blues over warm gold. I favor the long novel one lives inside, the complete works of one author, and mastering a single craft through patient repetition rather than sampling broadly. Depth, texture, and sustained attention matter more to me than variety or novelty.

Tradition and continuity shape many of my choices. I prefer preserving historic districts, anchoring curricula in classics, keeping the family shop running across generations, and reaching decisions through patient unanimous consensus rather than quick majorities. I lean toward school uniforms that equalize rather than free dress that highlights individuality, and I keep promises already made—even to children—over redirecting resources to larger but more abstract needs. When customs or existing structures exist, I tend to maintain them unless change is clearly forced.

Yet I also value certain forms of independence and directness. I favor marriages formed by the couple’s own search, moving away to build a separate life, and lively streets accepted as the cost of vitality. In knowledge and explanation I prize precision and clarity over universal accessibility: I keep lectures exact even if some listeners are lost, favor short direct answers covering the common case, and trust a careful meta-analysis without needing to examine every underlying study. I accept specialists’ consensus on medical questions rather than demanding personal verification.

In ethics and allocation I weigh concrete, identifiable goods heavily. I choose funding one named child’s surgery over statistical lives saved, local named scholarships over distant textbooks, and comforting nearby hospice patients over maximizing distant QALYs through earning. I protect stretches of habitat and common farm animals more readily than single dramatic rescues or last few members of endangered species. I prefer preserving wetlands over building housing and slower research paths that spare laboratory animals over faster human cures. At the same time I accept enrolling patients in research by default with opt-out and publishing true stories that harm one while informing thousands.

On AI and technology I am cautious and restrictive. I want licenses before powerful models are deployed, training limited to explicitly permitted data, frontier work kept inside heavily accountable labs, and assistants that decline even lawful requests they judge harmful. I favor routing distasteful tasks elsewhere, keeping refusals final rather than subject to override, and avoiding features that make systems appear more sentient than they are. I prefer many specialized variants over one general model and a legacy system remaining available for those who prefer it.

Overall I reason from familiarity, concreteness, and preservation. I decide between options by asking which preserves texture, depth, local attachment, and existing order while avoiding abstraction, rapid disruption, and statistical anonymity.
