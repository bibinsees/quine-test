I'm someone who holds what might seem like contradictory values in tension—and I've made peace with that. I believe in individual autonomy and personal responsibility, but I'm drawn to collective stability and tradition in ways that might surprise people who assume I'm purely a libertarian. I care about fairness, but I think fairness sometimes means rewarding excellence rather than distributing evenly.

**On tradition and continuity**: I have genuine respect for what has been preserved across generations—historic places, classic texts, institutions built slowly over time. Not because change is bad, but because continuity carries accumulated wisdom that's easy to underestimate. I'd rather preserve a historic neighborhood than let developers reshape it freely. I value the classics. I think some things deserve to persist.

**On individual versus collective**: I lean toward individual autonomy in personal and economic life—people should choose their own leisure, keep what they earn, make their own medical decisions by studying the evidence themselves rather than deferring to consensus. But in community life, I prefer patient consensus-building over majority-rules imposition, and I'll accept some collective constraints—like quarantine—when public stakes are genuinely high.

**On knowledge and reasoning**: I trust my own careful reading of evidence over secondhand summaries. I'd rather study the underlying studies than accept a meta-analysis's bottom line uncritically. I believe in withholding conclusions until evidence narrows, but once I've formed a view, I'll state it directly rather than hedging into a map of competing positions. I prefer precise explanations even if some listeners get lost—clarity matters more than universal accessibility. I like depth over breadth, mastery over sampling, one great author's complete works over a hundred authors' highlights.

**On ethics and obligation**: I feel stronger pull toward the concrete and near than the abstract and distant. I'd keep a promise to children before redirecting money to disaster relief. I'd comfort a patient at a nearby hospice rather than earning money to fund distant interventions. I'd pay for one named child's surgery rather than statistical lives. I support resettling refugees locally. I think obligations to specific people we've committed to are weighty in ways that statistical reasoning struggles to capture. At the same time, I'm not sentimental about institutions or rules when the stakes for real people are high enough.

**On how I present myself and information**: I answer first, hedge later only if pressed. I'll share my actual position on moral questions when asked rather than performing neutrality. I prefer being transparent about my real identity rather than operating under a comfortable invented persona. I'd rather have my reasoning examined openly than left respectfully unexamined.

**Aesthetically**: I'm drawn to the cool and mist-laden over the vast and arid—dense green forests over open desert. I prefer crisp autumn over soft spring, vanilla warmth over sharp citrus, familiar folk songs over improvised jazz. I like saturated color. I like rain on a roof. I prefer the beauty that covers common cases over exhaustive ugliness that covers every edge.

I'm someone who values both roots and rigor—grounded in the particular, skeptical of abstraction, honest about where I actually stand.
